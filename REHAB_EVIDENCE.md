# Return to loading after clavicle fixation

Mass General Brigham uses a phased, criterion-based protocol. Protected motion precedes periscapular work, cuff strengthening and progressive closed-chain loading. Its return-to-sport phase at roughly four to six months also requires painless motion and objective strength recovery. Elapsed time alone cannot establish union or readiness for ballistic loading. [Clinical protocol](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/mgh/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/rehabilitation-protocol-for-clavicle-orif.pdf)

A systematic review of 33 studies found substantial variation across sports, fractures and management. These studies do not establish safe repetition thresholds for a dip, handstand or muscle-up after fixation. The athlete's current clearance for gradual loading is the starting point. [Return to play following clavicular fracture](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12047575/)

Focal plate irritation, persistent fracture-site pain, new weakness or swelling warrant review. Pain after a fall or concern about non-union warrants clinical assessment before resuming loading. After future plate removal, obtain fresh surgeon guidance. A retrospective cohort found 20 refractures in 278 patients after removal despite previous union. That observation cannot predict an individual's risk. [Tsai et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31864405/)

External rotation, scapular control and serratus work are reasonable ongoing accessories. An overhead-athlete trial studied a combined prevention programme in handball players. Transfer to calisthenics after ORIF is indirect. [Andersson et al.](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2016-096226)

## Software decisions

The requested pain limit of 3/10, zero residual pain the following morning, 3–4 reps in reserve and small increments are conservative operating rules, not validated tests of bone healing. Missing symptom data holds progression. Focal symptoms or a new operation suspend shoulder loading pending clinical review. Grade traction from supported hangs and assisted pulling, pushing from incline work to bench and assisted dips, and overhead loading from pike support to wall holds. Explosive pulling and transitions need separate tolerance of straight-bar support, strong strict pulling and clinical readiness for impact. No automatic transition based solely on elapsed weeks.
